ASR issues and impact on feedback generation

The issueWhat happensWhat it does to feedbackEvidence
Word swapsA word becomes a different word: Sport Club → "sparkle"The learner is corrected for words they never saidChan et al. 2022; Choe et al. 2022; this study
The invisible fixThe recogniser silently cleans up the learner's real mistakes: they wants → "they want"The real mistake can never be taught — and the learner may be praised for grammar the recogniser producedSkidmore & Felice 2025, internal report; this study
Word-ending damageEndings (-s, -ed) are added or dropped: join → "joined"Grammar feedback aims at recogniser noise, not the learnerSkidmore & Felice 2025, internal report; Disney & Le 2024; Choe et al. 2022
Made-up speechThe recogniser invents whole phrases, especially around pausesInvented speech can be quoted back as the learner'sKoenecke et al. 2024
Wrong speakerThe learner repeats the bot, or speakers get merged — text alone can't tell who said whatThe learner is praised for the bot's wordsChan et al. 2022; this study
Missing speechQuiet or hesitant words simply vanish; confidence scores cannot see what was deletedLearners can be routed to generic feedback for the recogniser's failure, not their ownQiu et al. 2021; this study
Is it possible to provide personalised feedback, given the ASR challenge?

This study

106 real learner dialogues, each transcribed by the ASR and by human transcribers — so every quote in the generated feedback could be checked against what the learner really said.

Simple quoting requestGoverned quoting strategy
What it isThe live prompt plus one sentence: quote what the learner said. Nothing else changes.Quoting is allowed only under strict conditions with principles guiding when, what, and how to quote.
