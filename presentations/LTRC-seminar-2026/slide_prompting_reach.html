✓  Where the prompt reaches — three levels of realisation in practice
The LLM generates text; the TTS renders text-level markers as prosody. Three concrete examples from the AiBC prompt:
§5.3 ≥ 50% speaker time✓ FULLY REALISED
The LLM decides response length. "Use brief prompts rather than long speeches" lands directly.
Hesitation / thinking-pause markers✓ PARTIALLY REALISED
Claude · verbal prompt
"Talk like a real learner — some hesitation as you search for words, filled pauses ('um', 'well…'), occasional restarts, slightly shorter sentences."
AiBC · §4 Voice and Tone
"Start some turns with 'Oh' or 'Hmm' or 'Well' to create a natural thinking pause before your response."
Both ask for hesitation — AiBC's renders softer: markers scoped to turn-onset only (Claude hesitates mid-utterance), and Tier-1 TTS speaks disfluencies less expressively than Claude voice mode (Tier 2).
Persona & level framing✓ IMPLICITLY REALISED — via prosody
Persona:  "You are a friendly, approachable fellow student."
Level:  "Pitch your language at B1–B2 level…"
Why it lands: abstract style → measurable prosodic shift (Rabin et al., 2026: Cohen's d up to 4.24 across 22 TTS voices).
Prompting reaches text, prosodic markers, and even style-induced prosody.
